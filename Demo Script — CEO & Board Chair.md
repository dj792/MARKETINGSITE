# Walkthrough Script — CEO & Board Chair

**Runtime:** ~2:40 spoken, ~3:00 with pauses
**Audience:** Chamber/association CEO + Board Chair (the Chair doesn't know you)
**Examples, in order:** Board Meeting Dashboard → Membership vs. Budget → Profitability
**Close:** No ask. Plant the idea and stop.

---

## Delivery notes

- Talk slower than feels natural. The pauses are where they actually look at the screen.
- Say the Chair's name once, at the Board Meeting Dashboard. It's their meeting — hand it to them.
- Don't narrate every tile. Point at one number per screen and let the rest sit there.
- When you say "I'm not going to pitch you anything today," mean it. Then stop talking.

---

## 0:00 — Open *(before you share the first screen)*

> Before I show you anything, one sentence on why I care about this.
>
> I spent most of my career building software for chambers and associations — I founded WebLink, which grew into the membership platform for a few thousand chambers before it was acquired. And I've sat in the CEO chair myself.
>
> What I kept running into, on both sides of that: the strategy was never the hard part. The hard part was never quite being able to see, day to day, whether we were actually winning.

*Pause. Then share the screen.*

---

## 0:30 — Frame the three examples

> So what I want to show you isn't really software. It's what it looks like when a CEO and a board are working from the same numbers — and they trust them.
>
> Three examples. The numbers aren't yours. But the questions are.

---

## 0:47 — Example 1: The Board Meeting Dashboard

*Aimed at the Chair. Use their name.*

> [Chair's name], this one's yours. This goes up on the screen at the monthly board meeting.
>
> Bank balance. Months of runway. Revenue against budget. Membership by market. One screen.
>
> What changes isn't the reporting — it's the meeting. Nobody spends the first twenty minutes reconciling numbers. The board starts on the second question instead of the first. Not "what happened," but "what do we do about it."
>
> And that QR code puts the same financials on every board member's phone.

---

## 1:22 — Example 2: Membership vs. Budget

> This one's the CEO's.
>
> New membership against budget — by count and by dollars, month by month, with the variance.
>
> The value here isn't the chart. It's the timing. Most CEOs find out they missed the month after the month is already over. That's an autopsy. This is a read you get while there are still selling days left to do something about it.

---

## 1:50 — Example 3: Profitability

*Slow down. This is the one that lands.*

> And this is the one that tends to get quiet in the room.
>
> Profitability by member, by tier, by location. Which relationships actually carry the organization — and which ones cost more to serve than they bring in.
>
> This one takes real work behind the scenes — a couple of years of financials, honest expense allocation. But nearly every leadership team I've walked through this has found something they were certain of that turned out not to be true.

*Let it sit for a beat.*

---

## 2:20 — Close *(no ask)*

> That's the whole idea, really. Not more dashboards — better decisions.
>
> When the numbers are right, and current, and clear enough to explain in one sentence, the second-guessing stops. The CEO leads with more confidence. The board governs with more confidence. And everybody's pulling in the same direction.
>
> I'm not going to pitch you anything today. I just wanted you to see what's possible.

*Stop. Let them ask the next question.*

---

## If the Chair asks about your background

Land on the part that's relevant to them — that you've been in their seat — not the run of wins.

> Sure. I started building software young, sold my first company at seventeen. Spent about eighteen years as CEO of two venture-backed technology companies, and founded WebLink, the first online membership platform for associations — it's part of Momentive now. These days I work alongside CEOs rather than running the company myself, which honestly suits me better.

**"So are you a software company, or a consultant?"**

> Both, and that's deliberate. The platform gives you the visibility. But software can't tell you which numbers matter for your organization, or what to do when one of them moves. That part still takes somebody who's run one of these. You get both — otherwise the dashboards just become more noise.

**"How long does this take to stand up?"**

> Days, not months, for the first views. We're connecting systems you already have, not asking anyone to enter data twice.

---

## If you're running long

Cut in this order:

1. The QR code line in Example 1.
2. "Bank balance. Months of runway…" — name two of the four, not all four.
3. Example 2 down to the timing point. The autopsy line is the whole idea.

Never cut the Profitability example or the final three sentences.
